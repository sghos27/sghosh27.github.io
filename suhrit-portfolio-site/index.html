Fusion-based driver state monitoring

A drowsy-driving detector that cross-checks a camera against the driver’s seat and the car’s motion.

0.93AUC for the fused detector
474 msmean time from detection to alert

Camera-only driver monitoring struggles at night, in glare, and behind sunglasses, which is when drowsy driving is most dangerous. I built a Raspberry Pi system that fuses three signals: eye closure from an ArduCam running a YOLOv8 model, posture from an array of force-sensing resistors in the seat, and the car’s trajectory from an accelerometer. If one sensor gets fooled, the other two can still catch it.

I wrote the fusion algorithm that weighs the three together. Across 30 test sessions in different lighting and eyewear conditions, t-tests and Fisher’s exact tests confirmed that the posture and fatigue markers held up (p < 0.01).

Hardware
Raspberry Pi, ArduCam, force-sensing resistor seat array, accelerometer
Model
YOLOv8 for eye-closure detection, plus my sensor fusion algorithm
Validation
30 sessions; t-tests and Fisher’s exact tests, p < 0.01

DigiGuard

A wearable that mechanically resists finger tremors and adjusts to them as they happen.

84%average reduction in tremor magnitude
15people tested, 20 trials each

People with ALS or Parkinson’s often lose fine motor control in their fingers early, which makes buttons, utensils, and pens hard to use. Most low-cost devices resist the shaking with a fixed spring. We built DigiGuard around a servo-driven cord-and-pulley system, so the resistance responds to the tremor itself.

I led manufacturing: I designed and tested the mechanical dampening system and finalized the arm brace. We went through four versions of the mechanism, and the move to an automated cord-and-pulley design is what cut down false activations. Testing covered 15 people with 20 trials each, more than 1,400 movement data points, checked with Welch’s t-tests.

Hardware
Arduino, 360° servo, 3D-printed brace, finger-mounted accelerometer
My role
Manufacturing lead: dampening system and arm brace
Validation
15 participants, 20 trials each; Welch’s t-test on 1,400+ data points
